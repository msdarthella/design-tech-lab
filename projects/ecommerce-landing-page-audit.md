# E-commerce Landing Page Audit

A sample audit framework for reviewing e-commerce landing pages for brand consistency, usability, accessibility, and conversion clarity.

## Project overview

E-commerce landing pages need to do several things at once: communicate the offer or product story, support the brand, guide shoppers toward action, and work well across devices.

When landing pages are built as one-off designs without shared patterns or documentation, teams may create inconsistent experiences, repeat production work, or miss opportunities to improve clarity and usability.

This project outlines how I would approach auditing an e-commerce landing page from a visual design, brand system, and production workflow perspective.

## Audit goals

- Review visual and brand consistency
- Clarify page hierarchy and user flow
- Identify reusable layout or content patterns
- Improve accessibility-minded design decisions
- Reduce production friction for recurring landing page needs
- Support stronger e-commerce storytelling and conversion clarity

## Page areas to review

A landing page audit could include:

- Hero section
- Promotional message
- Product story or feature section
- Product grid or collection module
- Category navigation
- Call-to-action placement
- Social proof or review content
- Value proposition section
- FAQ or support content
- Footer and secondary navigation

## What I would review

### Visual hierarchy

- Is the most important message immediately clear?
- Does the page guide the user from awareness to action?
- Are headings, body copy, imagery, and calls to action organized clearly?
- Does the layout work across desktop and mobile?

### Brand consistency

- Does the page feel connected to the broader brand system?
- Are typography, color, imagery, spacing, and layout choices consistent?
- Are seasonal or campaign-specific visuals balanced with core brand standards?
- Are there one-off styles that could become reusable patterns?

### E-commerce usability

- Are products, offers, and calls to action easy to understand?
- Is the shopper’s next step clear?
- Are product modules or collection sections scannable?
- Does the page reduce decision friction?

### Accessibility considerations

- Is text readable across screen sizes?
- Is there enough contrast between text, backgrounds, and buttons?
- Are links and calls to action visually clear?
- Does the design avoid relying on imagery alone to communicate key information?

### Production workflow

- Which sections could become reusable templates or modules?
- Are there repeated layouts that should be documented?
- Would a Figma component or landing page template reduce future production time?
- Are handoff notes, asset specs, or naming conventions needed?

## Potential deliverables

- Landing page audit notes
- Reusable section inventory
- Brand consistency recommendations
- Accessibility checklist
- Figma component recommendations
- Mobile layout notes
- Campaign or seasonal template recommendations

## Example recommendations

- Standardize hero, product grid, value proposition, and CTA sections
- Create reusable Figma components for recurring landing page modules
- Define layout rules for campaign landing pages
- Add accessibility checks for contrast, typography, and CTA clarity
- Document image ratio and asset guidelines for faster production
- Create mobile-first review steps before launch

## Reflection

A strong e-commerce landing page should support both the shopper and the team creating it.

Clear hierarchy, reusable patterns, accessibility-minded decisions, and practical documentation can help teams create landing pages that feel on-brand, easier to maintain, and more effective over time.
